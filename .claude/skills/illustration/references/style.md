# Style block

Paste this block, unchanged, at the top of every prompt. Then add the composition.

```text
A line drawing in the style of a 19th century naturalist's plate (natural history, anatomy, botany, malacology), with the precision of a technical drawing when the subject is a mechanism.
Black ink only, on a fully transparent background. Fine, even pen lines with slightly heavier outlines.
Sparse, open line work: most surfaces stay white, hatching only on the shadow side and in cut sections, never an all-over engraved texture. Shading made exclusively with parallel hatching, cross-hatching and stippling; no solid fills, no grey wash, no gradients, no colour.
Precise observational draughtsmanship: specimens drawn as if from life, every structure described (chambers, membranes, segments, branches, veins).
Scientific views: cross-section, dissection, specimen shown from its most telling angle, several related specimens side by side; dashed lines for hidden parts and motion.
A single subject, centred, entirely within the frame, with generous empty space around it.
No text, no letters, no numbers, no reference numerals, no leader lines, no captions, no border, no frame, no title, no signature, no stamp.
No human figures, no faces, no hands (anatomical parts such as a neuron or a brain section are fine). Not a cartoon, not an icon, not a flowchart, not a diagram with boxes and arrows, not photorealistic. No paper texture.
```

# Composition guidelines

- Describe the object physically: its parts, how they connect, the view angle, what is cut away, what is exploded, what is dashed.
- One mechanism per drawing. If the idea needs two objects, show the relation physically (one feeds the other, one holds the other).
- Movement is shown by dashed trajectories or by exploded parts slightly apart, never by speed lines.
- Scale cues are mechanical (a screw, a handle), never human.
